The Schedule Fraud Has Requested That You Eat Shit

November 5, 2026 · All six Week 8 matchups final · Yahoo League 147988

IN THIS EDITIONThe Cold OpenThe Weekly Shitshow: All Six MatchupsSexual Fantasy Power Rankings: The Official Pecking OrderThe Roast & Awards DepartmentReceipts: Cooper’s Record Is Reporting Old NewsLeague Drama & DesperationNext Week: Who’s Getting Fucked?

Jake has spent enough time atop this league to develop a problem familiar to undefeated arguments and suspiciously cheap sushi: people keep asking what the catch is. Winning with 83.22 in Week 6 did not help. Neither did owning a record that looked considerably better than the underlying football.

Then he posted the highest score of Week 8. That complicates the prosecution.

Gibbs to the Crib is 7-1, and this time nobody needs to search the rulebook for how the hell it happened. Jake beat a team that actually scored points. He got production across the lineup. For one glorious week, the man accused of bringing a fake ID to the contenders’ table brought a passport.

That does not erase the schedule luck. It does make the next game delicious: first-place Jake meets scoring leader Connor, with neither able to hide behind the other’s résumé. Eight weeks of arguing over who is actually good have finally produced an appointment.

The Roast & Awards Department

Worst Bench Crime: Arturo, for the simplest possible alternative. Tyler Allgeier scored 36.10 on the bench. Bijan Robinson scored 7.60 in a starting RB slot. That one legal swap adds 28.50 and turns the result into a hypothetical 150.50-149.44 win. This does not require an elaborate FLEX conspiracy. Same position, one substitution. The winning ticket was in his pocket while he complained about the lottery.

The Unsupervised Escape Award: Nick. He left 31.40 legally recoverable points unused, the most of any manager this week, and won anyway. His lineup efficiency was 76.81%. Most people need to finish the exam to pass. Nick got a substitute teacher who forgot to collect it.

The Dependent Deduction: Alex. Burrow accounted for 42.47% of the winning score. London and Metcalf combined for 1.78%. At that point they are not receiving partners; they are two names Joe has to include on the Christmas card.

Receipts: Cooper’s Record Is Reporting Old News

The most interesting team outside the playoff places might be the one sitting tenth. Cooper’s first five weeks and last three weeks look like two different products sold under the same unfortunate brand name.

That recent scoring average leads all twelve teams. Alex is next at 122.65. Cooper has also increased his score in each of those three weeks: 117.04, 122.36, then 136.12. This is more than one loud Sunday being asked to do the work of a trend.

The warning label matters. Cooper’s season-long all-play record is still 41-47, and 3.73 expected wins are only modestly above his three actual wins. His overall record is not some historic miscarriage of justice. The argument is narrower: the early-season damage is hiding a much better recent team.

Mason provides the useful contrast. He has the better official record at 4-4, yet a 100.95 average and 10-23 all-play record over the same three weeks. Both remain in the race. One has been gathering momentum; the other has been gathering acceptable-looking paperwork.

Calling Cooper a championship favorite would be premature. Treating him like a convenient opponent would be fucking stupid.

League Drama & Desperation

Jake’s roster work deserves some credit alongside the scoreboard. His October 28 claims of Kenny Gainwell for $5 FAB and Pat Freiermuth for $4 produced 15.00 and 10.60 in his starting lineup. Add the October 26 Jacksonville pickup, and three recent additions supplied 50.60. The standings leader did not spend the week reclining on a favorable schedule and hoping someone else fucked up.

Connor’s November 1 Bears pickup supplied 13.00 in a game decided by less than that. That is a material contribution, although it does not prove every alternative defense would have cost him the game.

The new waiver cycle brings a different kind of commitment: Jack spent $20 on Adonai Mitchell and now has $5 FAB remaining. Cooper still has $100. Neither balance predicts a playoff berth, but they do establish who can still win an expensive bidding argument.

Six playoff places are available. Jake and Alex lead, Connor and Sam follow at 5-3, and five teams sit 4-4: Arturo, Nick, Jack, Rusty and Mason. Yahoo currently places Arturo fifth and Nick sixth. Cooper is one win behind that cluster; Hunter is 2-6 and Spencer 0-8. Those are positions, not clinches or elimination notices. With the trade deadline on November 28, there is still time to improve a roster before calling the schedule an asshole.
