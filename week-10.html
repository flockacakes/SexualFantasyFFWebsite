Alex Takes the Penthouse. Sam Leaves the Door Unlocked.

Last week, this publication picked Jake to beat Alex. Alex responded by taking first place, beating the prediction, and leaving the columnist holding a neatly folded bag of shit. Accountability is important. So is pretending nobody saved the screenshot.

The top-of-the-table showdown delivered an actual contest: two productive teams, one lead changing hands in the standings, and no convenient bench excuse for the loser. Alex now owns the league’s only 8-2 record. That does not automatically make him the strongest team, but it does make every argument against him sound increasingly like somebody complaining outside a restaurant they cannot get into.

Elsewhere, Nick beat the scoring leader, Jack upgraded from last week’s suspicious win to this week’s unmistakable ass-kicking, and Sam somehow won while leaving a quarterback-sized crater in his lineup. Four regular-season weeks remain. The playoff bubble is tightening, the trade deadline is approaching, and several managers are discovering that “my team is better than its record” has a remarkably short shelf life.

League Drama & Desperation

The playoff line now sits at 6-4: Connor, Nick and Jack occupy Yahoo’s fourth through sixth positions. Arturo and Mason are one win behind at 5-5; Rusty is another back at 4-6. Cooper and Hunter are 3-7. Four regular-season games remain, and the November 28 trade deadline leaves little room for leisurely roster improvement. These are current positions, not clinching certificates.

One confirmed small move mattered immediately: Alex added Buffalo on November 12 and started the Bills for 15.00. Nick’s $1 Colts acquisition produced 17.00 in his winning lineup. Those points count; neither fact alone proves the manager could not have won with another defense.

The transaction feed also lists a November 16 Sam–Rusty trade: Jonathan Taylor toward Sam, with Nico Collins, Deebo Samuel Sr. and Justice Hill toward Rusty. Its completion is not confirmed, so nobody gets a victory lap or a retrospective trade grade yet. The distinction is particularly useful here: Deebo’s Week 10 points were earned in Sam’s starting lineup, not retroactively transferred with a name on a transaction.

At the resource extremes, Cooper still has $100 FAB and Jack has $5. Cooper can win a bidding contest. Jack currently owns three more actual victories. Neither currency can purchase the other after the fact.
